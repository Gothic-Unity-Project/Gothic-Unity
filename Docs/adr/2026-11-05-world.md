# ADR: World - lightmaps, fires, particles, mod worlds

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-11-05
- Technical details: Docs/architecture.md (domains, adapters)

## Context

The original engine lights caves, mines and houses with lightmaps baked from their static lights; ours only had the dim or black vertex light there, and the mines had our sun and fog. Its fireplaces and torches burn by the scripts' mob routine (lit at night), ours burned all the time or not at all, and their flames sat at the world's origin. Its particle effects run at Gothic's rate; ours were cut to a few particles per second, so torches showed a few sparks and smoke was invisible. Two mod worlds (Mroczne Tajemnice's ancient temple, Renovation 2026's dragon island) don't load at all because of two bugs in ZenKit, the library that reads Gothic's files.

## Decisions

1. **Mod worlds that ZenKit can't read are repaired before or after loading, always.** A materials header that starts with a certain byte (266 materials) and a chunk whose stored length is too short are fixed in a copy of the world file; vanilla worlds are untouched. Not chosen: a setting to switch the repairs off (there is nothing to gain from a world that doesn't load). Follow-up: raise both bugs as an issue and a fix (pull request) at ZenKit, then remove our workaround.
2. **Light mapped polygons show Gothic's lightmaps, and objects standing there take the light of the floor below them.** A static light that is already in the lightmaps then lights only objects and characters. This is the one new developer setting, on by default: the lightmaps are one more texture in memory, which may matter on the Quest. Not chosen: brighter vertex light (Gothic's own data already has the right light).
3. **Indoor worlds (mines, temples) have no sun and no fog,** like the engine's indoor sky. Always on.
4. **Fireplaces and torches follow the scripts' mob routine; indoors they always burn,** touching one in VR lights it or puts it out until the routine switches, and far away their effects are off. Their flames, smoke and lights stand where the fire is.
5. **Particle effects run at Gothic's rate,** one-shot effects through their keyframes, looping ones (torches, camp fires, ground fog) at their full rate up to a cap; smoke and fog are drawn, a texture the game doesn't have shows no white squares, trails have Gothic's width, particle controllers show their effect (G2's teleport platforms).
6. **Child objects of the world stand at their own place.** Gothic stores their position in the world, not relative to the object they belong to; before, they sat at their parent (Mroczne Tajemnice's sacks on a cart lay 17 m away). Fire trees are the exception: their parts are relative to the fire.
7. **Mod textures without enough mip levels get them generated,** one material per texture group, separate meshes for the two texture modes, and leaves and bushes (materials without collision) don't block, like the engine.
8. **All of these fixes are always on, without developer settings.** Main's own handling of rooms and of child objects' loaders stays; only what main lacked comes from the author's branch.

## Consequences

- The world cache is built again once (new lightmap and texture data); the first load takes longer.
- Cost on the Quest, not measured yet (the author plays on PCVR): the world's fire and torch lights are now computed per pixel instead of per vertex, also without lightmaps, and particle effects emit far more particles. The Quest gets lower particle caps for now; a Quest build has to show whether the per-pixel lights need a cheaper path there (follow-up).
- Nothing new is saved: fire states follow the routine again after loading, rain follows the player.
- The game loads Mroczne Tajemnice and Renovation; mod object types ZenKit doesn't know (item slots, dummy generators) are still skipped by ZenKit itself - noted for the same upstream follow-up.
- Not in this record: a torch held in the hand (lit with the trigger), music zones, reverb and the other sound changes, earthquakes - later PRs.
